Competitor Study: Alo Yoga

From the store floor, I can see who walks in. I wanted to understand who else they shop with, and why.

The market

lululemon is the reference point for premium technical apparel, built on decades of trust in athletic engineering and performance fabrics. But the space around it is getting crowded.

Top competitors

  • Alo YogaStreetwear-first styling that frames activewear as a status symbol, fast influencer marketing, and a large overlap with active lululemon shoppers.
  • VuoriPremium versatility from gym to office, wholesale partners like Nordstrom and Equinox, and a strong menswear heritage.
  • AthletaAn inclusive, multi-generational brand with a wide size range and values-driven positioning.

Emerging competitors

  • OnExtending its footwear authority into performance apparel.
  • Offline by AerieA fast-growing active legging brand at a lower price point.
  • Nike × SkimsNike's research and design with Skims silhouettes and drop culture, aimed at the same core shopper.
  • FableticsA membership model whose subscription cash flow funds new retail stores.

Alo Yoga up close

I used Michael Porter's competitor analysis framework to look at Alo from four angles: what they do, what they believe, what they're strong at and where they want to go.

  • StrategyOpening stores close to ours, marketing through wellness and luxury retreats rather than fitness, shoppable influencer content, a VIP loyalty program, and owned yoga studios that bring people into the brand.
  • StrengthsA strong marketing engine with celebrity pull, studios that act as community anchors, and leadership hired from luxury fashion.
  • WeaknessesA heavy reliance on the U.S. market, and guest reviews that point to durability concerns at a luxury price.
  • BeliefsThe complete monochrome set is the new hero product. Fast, limited colour drops beat seasonal calendars. Activewear is now an everyday uniform, not just gym wear.
  • GoalsGrowing internationally with flagship stores, and becoming a wellness house, not only an apparel brand.

What I see on the floor

Putting the market next to what guests tell me in the store, these are the opportunities I keep coming back to:

  • Complete looksGuests often buy one piece at a time. Showing head-to-toe outfits and colour stories could make it easier to buy the whole look.
  • Comfort firstGuests are looking for 100% comfort, even in what they wear to work or out to dinner.
  • Men's lifestyleIn my experience, when men find something that works for them, they stick with it for years. That loyalty makes men's lifestyle a huge opportunity, with room for pieces like a sport jacket, an overshirt or shirt jacket, a clean button-down long sleeve, and a light, smart layer for the office, like a quilted vest or a minimal bomber.
  • Speed to trendWhen a silhouette goes viral, like the barrel leg, getting it to the floor quickly is how a brand owns the moment.
  • LifestyleGuests already wear us beyond the gym. There's room for more elevated, off-duty pieces in premium fabrics.

Method

Built from store locations, product drops, executive hires, annual reports and industry press, including lululemon's 2025 Annual Report, Business of Fashion, Modern Retail, CNBC and Earnest Analytics, combined with my own observations from the store floor.
