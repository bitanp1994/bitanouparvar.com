Competitor Study

A look at who lululemon competes with in premium athletic and lifestyle apparel, from established rivals to fast-growing newcomers. Then a closer look at Alo Yoga: its strategy, strengths, weaknesses, assumptions and goals, and what it means on the floor.

01 / The market

Who lululemon competes with

LULULEMON

The reference point for premium technical apparel, footwear and community retail: $11.1B FY25 net revenue and 811 company-owned stores across 30 countries.

Top competitors

1

ALO YOGA

$10B valuation

Streetwear-first styling that makes activewear a status symbol, fast influencer marketing, and a 63% overlap with active lululemon shoppers.

2

VUORI

$5.5B valuation

Gym-to-office versatility, wholesale partners like Nordstrom and Equinox, and a strong menswear heritage.

3

ATHLETA

−11% comps, Q1 FY26

An inclusive, multi-generational brand with sizes 0 to 3X and values-driven positioning.

Emerging competitors

1

ON

Footwear first

Extending its running authority into apparel, with a 47.7% apparel revenue surge.

2

OFFLINE BY AERIE

Accessible

The #2 active legging brand in the U.S., with 25% year-over-year revenue growth.

3

NIKE × SKIMS

Drop culture

Nike's research with Skims silhouettes, aimed at the same core shopper.

4

FABLETICS

Membership

2.7 million VIP members whose subscription cash flow funds 40 new retail stores.

02 / Alo Yoga up close

A closer look at Alo Yoga

Five angles from Michael Porter's competitor analysis framework.

Current strategy

  1. 1
    The proximity play

    84% of Alo's U.S. stores sit within half a mile of a lululemon, a deliberate shadow of lululemon's premium foot traffic.

  2. 2
    Hospitality and elite activations

    Luxury destination partnerships, like the 7-Day Reset Ritual filmed at Chenot Palace Weggis in Switzerland, replace standard fitness marketing and place the brand inside high-end lifestyle spaces.

  3. 3
    Frictionless social commerce

    Direct click-to-buy links behind every influencer, blog and ambassador post.

  4. 4
    The "VIP" hook

    The Alo Access loyalty program uses premium language (VIP, A-List, All Access) to build emotional retention, with points redeemed for accessories.

  5. 5
    Horizontal expansion

    Moving into high-margin categories beyond apparel: skincare, wellness supplements and recovery footwear.

  6. 6
    Owned community spaces

    Its own yoga studios act as community anchors that feed the retail pipeline.

Sources: Hosted Aesthetics, Alo blog, Alo Access, Athletech News

Strength

  1. 1
    A high-impact marketing engine

    A viral marketing pipeline backed by private capital and highly visible celebrity fans, such as Kendall Jenner, Bella Hadid and Hailey Bieber.

  2. 2
    Closed-loop community anchors

    Yoga studios in prime city locations create a real community that naturally sends traffic to the stores.

  3. 3
    Luxury design leadership

    Hiring the former CEO of Miu Miu signals the goal: to operate like a luxury fashion house, not a gym brand.

Sources: Business of Fashion, Forbes, Schön!, Marie Claire

Weakness

  1. 1
    Heavy reliance on the U.S.

    Still mostly U.S. sales despite new stores in London and Paris, leaving it more exposed to North American shifts than lululemon's more diversified base.

  2. 2
    A product durability gap

    Widespread guest complaints about pilling, loose stitching and peeling logos suggest the garment engineering doesn't yet match the luxury price.

Sources: Trustpilot

Assumptions

  1. 1
    The set is the new flagship

    Beyond core lines like Airlift and Airbrush, Alo believes the head-to-toe monochrome uniform drives the biggest baskets.

  2. 2
    Rule-breaking rules the market

    Fast, limited colour drops instead of seasonal calendars and clearance, to sell at full price.

  3. 3
    Activewear is a status uniform

    People no longer buy activewear for the gym, but to be seen in every day.

  4. 4
    Legacy brands are "trapped in the gym"

    Alo assumes older competitors are too focused on performance to win the daily lifestyle market.

Future goals

  1. 1
    Prepare for a public offering

    Streamlining the business for a high-value IPO or major equity sale.

  2. 2
    Grow internationally

    Permanent flagship "sanctuaries" in Europe, the UK and Greater China.

  3. 3
    Become a global luxury house

    Positioning alongside fashion houses, supported by senior luxury talent.

Sources: Inc., Modaes

My method: reverse-engineered from their store locations, product drop layouts and executive hires.

03 / What I see on the floor

What guests are looking for

After the market and Alo, here is what I hear from guests every day about what they look for in athletic and lifestyle apparel.

1

COMPLETE LOOKS

Outfits, not pieces

Guests respond to head-to-toe outfits and colour stories that make it easy to picture, and buy, the whole look.

2

COMFORT FIRST

100% comfort

Guests want comfort even in what they wear to work or out to dinner, which means soft, stretchy fabrics in tailored cuts.

3

SPEED TO TREND

Own the moment

When a silhouette goes viral, like the barrel leg, guests start asking for it within weeks.

4

MEN'S LIFESTYLE

Loyal for years

When men find what works, they stick with it. Many ask for pieces that move from work to the weekend: a sport jacket, overshirt, button-down, knit polo, easy casual pants and a light office layer.
