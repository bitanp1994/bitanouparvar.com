## The problem

While doing visual merchandising, I noticed that our day-to-day choices relied heavily on creative intuition. There was a clear gap between the numbers and how we executed on the floor. I didn't see the floor only as a store; I saw it as a living database.

## What I built

I asked for our store's Power BI metrics and started translating the raw weekly numbers into simple, structured reports, so my team could make informed choices on the floor and turn daily habits into measurable results.

<ul class="specs">
  <li><span class="label">Weekly report</span><span>A five-minute visual snapshot that turns Power BI data into daily targets, grouping performance by KPI and top-selling products, so busy floor teams can act on real numbers instead of guesswork.</span></li>
  <li><span class="label">Guest feedback</span><span>A quick feedback tool called <em>Limu</em> that lets team members log why guests leave empty-handed while the observation is still fresh, capturing what standard dashboards miss.</span></li>
  <li><span class="label">Local VM</span><span>Adapting head office visual merchandising guidelines to our store's demand, so displays can be repositioned to match what guests are actually buying.</span></li>
  <li><span class="label">Back of house</span><span>Using sell-through data to reorganize storage, with the fastest-moving styles and sizes in a dedicated "golden zone" so the inventory team can prioritize pulls.</span></li>
  <li><span class="label">Escalation</span><span>Flagging low sell-through items to the inventory team, creating a path from floor data to store leadership and head office.</span></li>
</ul>

## Impact

The report became the baseline the store management team uses for its weekly and monthly business reviews. I also briefed our Regional Manager on its structure and method, taking a store-level solution into a wider conversation.

## What the floor taught me

Working with this data every week, I started to see patterns in the assortment itself.

<ul class="specs">
  <li><span class="label">Complete outfits</span><span>We focus on selling standalone pieces rather than full head-to-toe looks. Competitors design and colour-drop complete capsules, making it effortless to buy the whole outfit.</span></li>
  <li><span class="label">Fit expansion</span><span>Extending lines into endless rises and cuts instead of new silhouettes cannibalizes sales between near-identical items, adds SKU work and dilutes floor freshness.</span></li>
  <li><span class="label">Speed to market</span><span>Fresh styles reach the floor slowly, so faster competitors own the moment when a silhouette goes viral, as with the barrel-leg trend.</span></li>
  <li><span class="label">Lifestyle tier</span><span>We are strong in technical gear, but there is room for a higher-priced lifestyle line: considered streetwear cuts and premium fabrics for off-duty outfits.</span></li>
</ul>
