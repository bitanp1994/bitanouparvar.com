Weekly Insights Report

A one-page weekly report that turns sales data into something the team can read in five minutes and act on.

The gap

In our store, everyday decisions rarely looked at the numbers. Which products to feature, how to style the mannequins, what to move to the front and what to restock were mostly decided by feel, even though the sales data was there. I suggested closing that gap by translating our data into simple weekly insights and stories, so the floor team, the VM team and management could all use them in their daily decisions.

The report

My team talks to guests all day, and most of them don't love numbers. So every section has one job, and the titles alone tell the week's story.

1234567
  1. The headlineOne big idea for the week. If someone only reads this line, they still know what matters.
  2. Five numbersEach comes with a plain-language line, like "about 1 in 7 guests bought", and last week underneath for context.
  3. Day by dayHow busy each day was and whether it beat its goal, so the team knows when to expect the rush.
  4. WomenWhat moved since last week, and the styles guests chose most. Only the story is in colour; the rest stays grey.
  5. MenThe same two views for men, so educators know what to talk about and what to pair together.
  6. MomentumEvery category sorted by comparing it with last year and last week: accelerating, recovering, under pressure or at risk.
  7. Ideas and your turnSuggestions from the data, framed as ideas to try. The QR code opens Limu, where the team shares what guests asked for.

The Weekly, Week 40. The story is real; the numbers have been changed to protect store data.

The check-in

Numbers show what sold, but they can't show why a guest walked out empty-handed. So I built Limu, a short weekly check-in the team fills in on their phones in about two minutes. A QR code on the report leads straight to it.

  • It asks what guests couldn't find, why they left without buying, and what got in the way that week.
  • Before the new questions, it shows what the team said the week before, so everyone can see their answers being used.
  • A lemon mascot keeps it light, so people actually come back to it.

Screens from Limu, with example answers.

The impact

The whole team uses the report to create a better guest experience, and each group puts it to work in its own way:

  • ManagementThe starting point for weekly and monthly business recaps.
  • InventorySpotting fast and slow sellers week over week, flagging low sell-through styles to the inventory team, and using guest feedback to surface size and stock gaps.
  • Back of houseReorganizing the golden zone so the fastest-moving styles are closest to hand.
  • Visual merchandisingDeciding which products to prioritize and how to merchandise them.
