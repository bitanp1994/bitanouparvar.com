Weekly Insights Report

A two-page weekly report that turns sales data into something a busy team can read in five minutes and act on.

Why I made it

Our team had access to a sales dashboard full of useful numbers, but it was rarely opened and almost never part of the conversation. Choices about what to feature, where to place product and what to restock were made mostly on instinct. I wanted to close that gap by putting the right numbers in front of the team, in plain language, every week.

What's inside

Every number comes with a short label that explains it, and every page ends with something to do.

How I built it

Each week I pull the numbers from the store's Power BI dashboard and keep only the few that help with decisions on the floor. Then I add what the numbers can't show: what guests ask for in the fitting room, what they can't find, and how product is landing on the floor. The data says what happened; the floor helps explain why.

Limu: the floor check-in

Numbers show what sold, but they can't show why a guest walked out empty-handed. So I built Limu, a short weekly check-in the team fills in on their phones in about two minutes. A QR code on the report leads straight to it.

  • It asks what guests couldn't find, why they left without buying, and what got in the way that week.
  • Before the new questions, it shows what the team said the week before, so everyone can see their answers being used.
  • A lemon mascot keeps it light, so people actually come back to it.

Screens from Limu, shown with example answers.

How it's used

The report became the starting point for the management team's weekly and monthly business recaps, and I walked our Regional Manager through how it's structured and why.

What I learned

  • Data only helps when people can read it in minutes. Fewer numbers, explained well, beat a full dashboard.
  • Week-over-week comparisons tell a better story than a single total.
  • Every insight needs an action next to it, or it gets forgotten.
  • Numbers and guest conversations work best together. Each one fills the other's blind spot.
