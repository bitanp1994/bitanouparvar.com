Since I started reading Derek Guy's posts on menswear, I pay much more attention to how pants fit the men I help in the fitting room.

What I've noticed: in about 60 to 70 percent of cases, men barely check the fit at all. They look at two things, the length and the waist. And most of the time, as soon as the pants are on, their hands go straight into the pockets.

But the back matters just as much as the front. How the pants sit over the seat tells you a lot: too tight, and the fabric pulls; too loose, and it sags. A good fit should drape cleanly from the seat down to the leg.

Now, when someone tries on a pair, I'm learning to gently ask them to turn around and look at the back in the mirror. It takes a few extra seconds, and it can be the difference between pants they wear once and pants they keep for years.
